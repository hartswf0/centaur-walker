#8-32 ROD RETAINED · FITS NOT VERIFIED

Change one condition.

Three short experiments
  1. Bare metal: turn once. A restrained nut moves 0.794 mm relative to the rod.
  2. Sleeved support: turn once. Observe the stationary blue bushings and the rotating purple tube. Nut travel is zero.
  3. Lost drive: turn once. The green input moves, but the rod and output do not. Bearings cannot fix a slipping hub.

The proposed stack, with its missing fits exposed.

The sleeve is a rigid metal tube spanning both supports. End washers bear on that tube, not on the stationary brackets. A separate hub grips the tube at the green input. The threaded rod remains inside and carries rotation to the output through the end-clamp connection.

Concept only: the tube, bushings and green clamping hub are not confirmed parts you own. No welding is shown. A bushing must be securely fitted in a suitable support; the existing strap holes may be too small. Do not buy from the illustrative dimensions below.

What to do on the real assembly next
  1. With tire drive disengaged, mark a line across the green part, washer and rod end. Gently turn the green part and see which marks stay together.
  2. Measure the two support holes and the distance between their outside faces. These determine whether bushings and a sleeve can fit.
  3. Back the rubbing nut/washer away from the fixed strap for a hand test. If it turns more freely, that identifies face rubbing; it does not replace a bearing.
  4. Before connecting the leg, verify the support is rigid, the rod cannot slide out, and every intended drive connection follows in both directions without slipping.
What this model does and does not prove

It computes thread travel from relative rod–nut rotation. It illustrates ideal sticking/slipping states and separates rotating parts from the frame. An unlocked nut with no contact is treated as co-rotating for comparison; its real behavior depends on friction and vibration.

It does not predict friction, starting torque, shaft bending, bearing life, clamping force, plastic creep, belt slip, or leg load. Nuts locked to the rod can still rub and bind against a stationary support. Washer clearance and sleeve end-clamp strength must be established on the actual build.
