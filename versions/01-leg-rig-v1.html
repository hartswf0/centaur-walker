Jansen linkage

Theo Jansen's leg has one degree of freedom: the crank angle sets every joint. The dashed curve is this rig's near hind toe path scaled to the same stride width. The dial lists where each leg's crank throw sits for the selected gait; on a crankshaft, those throw angles are the gait.

What the rig is showing

  • One fingerThe horse stands on the tip of its third digit. The fetlock is the knuckle, the hoof is the nail. Below the carpus and hock there is no muscle, only bone, tendon and ligament, so the lower leg is light and swings fast.
  • NamesThe front "knee" is the wrist (carpus). The hock is the ankle; the point of hock is the heel. The stifle is the true knee, tucked up against the belly. Turn on "Human names" to see the mapping.
  • Hind couplingThe reciprocal apparatus ties hock flexion to stifle flexion with two tendinous bands, so the middle of the hind leg moves as one degree of freedom, close to a parallelogram. In the rig, hock angle follows stifle angle at a fixed ratio. A Jansen leg is fully 1-DOF; the hind limb is partly so.
  • Fore lockIn stance the carpus locks straight, so the forearm and cannon act as one strut. It only folds in swing, and it folds backward. Turn on "Tie" to see the hind coupling cord and its length drift through the stride.
  • Fetlock springUnder load the fetlock sinks as the suspensory ligament stretches, then returns energy at toe-off. Watch the pastern flatten at mid-stance; the drop grows with speed.
  • Mass up topThigh, croup, shoulder and arm carry the muscle. For a costume or puppet, put bulk and weight in the upper parts and keep the cannon and pastern thin, or the leg will swing sluggishly.
  • ScapulaThe horse has no collarbone. The shoulder blade slides and rotates on the ribcage, held by muscle, so the front leg's top pivot is itself moving. In the rig it is a free angle the solver uses.

More ideas like this

  • Gait hubDrive four puppet legs from one crankshaft. Swap a hub whose throws sit at 0°, 90°, 180°, 270° (walk) for one at 0° and 180° pairs (trot). The hub is a physical gait selector.
  • Hock cordOn a brad puppet, run a thread from the front of the thigh just above the stifle to the top front of the hind cannon. Pulling the thigh forward then folds the hock automatically, which is the reciprocal apparatus in string.
  • Carpus stopGlue a small tab behind the front cannon top so the carpus cannot bend forward. It only folds back, and it holds straight under load.
  • Fetlock bandBridge the fetlock with a rubber band so the pastern sinks and springs back when the puppet is pressed down.
  • Heel driveFor a wearable rear half, a cord from the wearer's heel to the puppet's crank gives one stride per human stride. A human walk is two beats, so a 1:1 drive reads as a trot; a 2:1 gear gives a walk.
  • RotoscopeLoad other Muybridge plates (walk, trot, a centaur-scale cow) into Plate 2 and fit the offset; the slider value you settle on is a measured phase for that animal.
  • Centaur seatIn the Centaur body mode the human pelvis sits where the horse's neck root would be, above the front legs. That puts the human's weight on the forelimbs, which already carry about 60% of a horse's weight.
