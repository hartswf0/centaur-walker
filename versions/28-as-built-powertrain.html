Measure diameters • millimetres

Use effective belt-running diameters, not flange diameters. Red tire-contact diameter and red belt-takeoff diameter are separate. A belt needs a continuous running surface; the bare paint-roller wires are not one.

Measure shaft positions • millimetres

Side view: X is distance rearward along the channel; Y is height above channel. Z is across the bike. All shaft axes must be parallel. Defaults are rough layout estimates, not ruler readings accurate enough to order belts.

The two orange pulley lanes must be rigidly coupled to the same rotating cage/drum. The orange handle and bent wire frame stay stationary.

Belt length and direction

0 = unknown. Enter the chosen belt’s actual requirement. Geometric loop lengths are not ordering/cut lengths: account for belt pitch line, installed stretch and take-up using its maker’s instructions. Crossed belts need a suitable belt type and clearance at the crossing.

Stop the axle unscrewing

Current failure: a rotating threaded rod cannot run through a stationary nut as a bearing. It advances by 25.4 / 32 = 0.794 mm per relative revolution. If nuts merely rub the stationary plates, drag can also turn them relative to the rod.

The long #8-32 rod is not approved here as a loaded drive shaft. Putting larger washers or locknuts on it does not fix bending, an oversized support hole, or a missing torque connection.

Today's sequence
  1. Disengage from the tire. Secure the bicycle for a stationary hand test. Tie back or remove every loose cord and hook near the spokes.
  2. Fix the platform. Bolt/clamp the channel to structural rack rails with backing plates; bungees and basket wires are not the drive mount.
  3. Fix the top bearing arrangement. Pick live shaft or rotating tube, not a mixture. Put supports close to pulley and crank loads. Cap exposed ends.
  4. Prove the red takeoff. Attach a concentric pulley to the rotating hub/body, not to its stationary mounting bolt. Do not drill or clamp a bearing race or tire tread. If there is no sound mounting face, replace that takeoff assembly.
  5. Prove any orange stage. Turn its input pulley and restrain the output gently with the drive disconnected. Both must move as one; the handle stays still. Add a continuous grooved drum or bypass this stage.
  6. Measure the final route. Align lanes, measure shaft centers and pulley running diameters, then choose the belt and provide adjustable center distance or an idler.
  7. Test unloaded, then one suspended leg. Hand-turn ten cycles. Add witness marks across hub/shaft and nuts. Stop for walking nuts, bent shaft, wandering belts or rubbing.
  8. Direction first, speed second. Advance the bicycle wheel by hand and confirm the hoof moves backward relative to the frame on its low stroke. Only after that, test the second leg and intended speed with guards in place.

These checks are a bench sequence, not a roadworthiness approval. If the powertrain cannot pass today, a suspended hand-cranked demonstration remains a useful finished outcome.

Belts to source

Purpose-made round urethane belting with compatible grooved pulleys is a candidate. Get a premade loop or the correct weld/connector system. Match minimum bend diameter and load, and allow a tension adjustment. No local inventory is confirmed.

Paracord: useful for laying out the route and a low-speed hand experiment. Knots cause a lump; it is not a confirmed continuous power belt. Do not melt paracord ends together as a structural splice.

Scrap inner tube: possible low-load bench experiment with a seamless loop of suitable length and proper pulleys; unknown creep/tracking/load capacity. It is not a substitute for securing the hubs and shafts.

Vacuum/appliance belt: only if its actual profile, loop length and minimum bend diameter fit; an arbitrary rubber ring is not a verified match.
