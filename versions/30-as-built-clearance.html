Why the old shaft loosens

A #8-32 rod is Ø4.17 mm. A stationary nut on that rotating rod produces 0.794 mm of axial travel per relative revolution. A nut or washer rubbing a stationary strap can also turn relative to the rod.

Threads are not a bearing surface. Support near each crank reduces its overhang; adding a random third bearing can cause binding if the bearings do not line up.

608 bearing example: 8 mm bore × 22 mm outside × 7 mm wide. This is not confirmed to be inside your wheels. A #8 rod has 3.83 mm diametral clearance in an 8 mm bore. Tape or extra washers do not make a fitted shaft.

Alternative: fix the axle and rotate a rigid torque tube on bearings around it. This needs compatible tube-end bearings and a way to secure both cranks to that tube; soft plumbing tubing does not substitute for it.

Leg hole centers · v14 reference

F is called B in v14. O is the crank-shaft center; C is the moving crank pin. This naming has not yet been matched to your marked physical leg. Enter hole-center distances, not overall stick lengths.

White plate 1 = F–D–P. White plate 2 = E–G–H. Wooden links = C–D, C–E, F–E and P–G. Plate outlines are visual approximations; hole centers define motion. A label like “327 GASKIN FG” must be mapped to this naming before changing lengths.
